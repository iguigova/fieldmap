Fieldmap

What the field holds, and which parts of it you haven't read yet.

What this is

It's hard to spot a gap in your reading when you've never heard of the subfield. This map is built from the papers themselves, not from anyone's list. Load your reference library, and the subfields you've never read show up as gaps.

Any topic

For a topic in any field, type it, or a paper's DOI, and OpenAlex gives its reading path: reviews to start with, a reading order, its most-cited papers and its new ones. Its works are grouped by the papers they cite, on a small map with a reading order for each group.

Your browser asks OpenAlex directly. A topic uses about 13 of the 1,000 credits OpenAlex gives each connection a day for free, and a DOI about 5, plus one for each of its groups, at most eight.

Your reading

Zotero, Mendeley and most reference managers export BibTeX. The file stays in your browser. Only the DOIs of papers that aren't on the map are sent, to OpenAlex, to look up what those papers cite.

How it's made

Papers are grouped by the words in their titles and abstracts and by the papers they cite. Each group is named by the words that set it apart, so the names are rough. A paper without an abstract is placed afterwards, by the papers it cites and failing that by its title.

A paper in your file is matched by its DOI when it's on the map. Otherwise it's placed by the papers it cites, looked up on OpenAlex, and failing that by its title words. Both are rough.

Each subfield says how many of four reruns found it again. A rerun clusters the same papers from a different random start, and finds a subfield again when one of its groups shares at least half of the papers in either. A subfield found again in at most half the reruns is soft: its edges depend on the start more than on the papers.

Each subfield names its five most frequent authors, institutions and funders on its papers from the map's last five years, as OpenAlex records them.

Each subfield lists the map's journals its papers appeared in, most first. For each journal, OpenAlex gives its fee for the latest year it has, whether it's fully open access and whether it's in MEDLINE. For a journal that isn't fully open access, the fee is the price of making one article open. The Directory of Open Access Journals gives a listed journal's licences and whether it waives the fee; for a journal it doesn't list, neither is known here. Fees change every year, so each list says the day it was fetched, and each fact links to where it came from.

A subfield's nearest are the three whose papers it cites and is cited by most, scaled by the two subfields' sizes so that two large ones are not near just for being large.

Each subfield's reading order is the ten of its papers that its own papers cite most, each placed after the ones of the ten it cites, the most cited first otherwise. Its new papers, and its reviews from any journal, are asked of OpenAlex when their list is opened: the newest works, and the most-cited reviews, citing the five works the subfield cites most tellingly, kept when they place in the subfield by what they cite.

A topic's reading path comes from one page of OpenAlex's search for it, or from a paper and the works citing it. Its reading order is the ten works those cite most, ordered the same way. Its reviews are those a topic's search found, then any other review it holds or that cites at least two of the five works it cites most, and its new papers are the works from the last twelve months citing at least two of those five. Either list puts the works citing more of the five first.

A topic's groups are the communities its works form when two works are linked by the papers they both cite, each weighted by how few of the works cite it; Louvain's method finds them. A group is shown when it holds five works or more, at most eight. Each is named by the title words that set it apart, and its reading order is the ten works its own works cite most.

The groups come from k-means on each paper's words and citations, set to groups. Every step and setting is in the method. The code was written with Claude, an AI assistant.
