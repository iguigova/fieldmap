Fieldmap: biostatistics

What the field holds, and which parts of it you haven't read yet.

What this is

It's hard to spot a gap in your reading when you've never heard of the subfield. This map is built from the papers themselves, not from anyone's list. Load your reference library, and the subfields you've never read show up as gaps.

Your reading

Zotero, Mendeley and most reference managers export BibTeX. The file stays in your browser. Only the DOIs of papers that aren't on the map are sent, to OpenAlex, to look up what those papers cite.

How it's made

Every article from eight general biostatistics journals, from OpenAlex. Papers are grouped by the words in their titles and abstracts and by the papers they cite. Each group is named by the words that set it apart, so the names are rough.

A paper in your file is matched by its DOI when it's on the map. Otherwise it's placed by the papers it cites, looked up on OpenAlex, and failing that by its title words. Both are rough. Tested on a fifth of the map's own papers, held back while the rest built the subfields, citations picked the paper's own subfield two times in three, and title words about half the time.

Each subfield says how many of four reruns found it again. A rerun clusters the same papers from a different random start, and finds a subfield again when one of its groups shares at least half of the papers in either. A subfield found again in at most half the reruns is soft: its edges depend on the start more than on the papers.

Each subfield names its five most frequent authors, institutions and funders on its papers from the map's last five years, as OpenAlex records them. OpenAlex sometimes credits a paper to the wrong person of the same name: of 58 recent papers it credits to Yale's Fan Li, one is by Duke's Fan Li, and one by both credits both to Yale's. It names a funder and its parts separately: of 581 recent papers that name the National Cancer Institute, 397 also name the National Institutes of Health, and only those count for both.

A subfield's nearest are the three whose papers it cites and is cited by most, scaled by the two subfields' sizes so that two large ones are not near just for being large.

The groups come from k-means on each paper's words and citations, set to 40 groups. Every step and setting is in the method. The code was written with Claude, an AI assistant.
